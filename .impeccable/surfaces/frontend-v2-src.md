## Direction contract

Scope: whole app (front door = Persuade; traveller and agent workspaces = Operate). Code-led, no image generation. Chosen by the user from the formal round: The Printed Timetable (the roll's assignment). Replaces the earlier Modular Identity world; the Delhi photographs and the real Leaflet maps stay.

THESIS: A trip is a printed timetable: ruled columns of times and stops, and every revision is a correction printed in the margin, never a highlight. It refuses the playful colour-bar poster look and the soft-card travel app.

OWN-WORLD: White paper (#ffffff) on a pale cool page ground (#f2f4f6), ink #14181d, hairline 1px rules with a 2px ink rule under headers, near-square corners (3px), no shadows, no colour bands. Four restrained signals that always mean something: timetable red #c4231a = withdrawn or needs attention, forest #1f6b35 = added or approved, navy #17407f = action and AI, amber #8a5a00 = pending. Archivo at weight 700 and normal width, tabular numerals for every time and amount, sentence-case headings, small caps-free labels. Photographs only as small captioned plates with a 1px frame; the map is a real OpenStreetMap inset. The chosen version is drawn solid and earlier versions dashed.

STORY: A visitor reads one day as a timetable and sees, in the margin, what version 2 withdrew and added and why. Travellers read and give feedback; agents compare options in ruled columns and approve. They enter the demo.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Masthead with the name and a ruled nav. Left seven columns: the headline "Plans that explain their own changes." at 56px, one sentence, a navy "Explore the demo" button and a quiet "Sign in". Right five columns: a framed plate with a Delhi photograph and caption above a real map inset. Directly under, the full width of the fold, a ruled timetable of Day 3 with a correction column.
